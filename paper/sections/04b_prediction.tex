\subsection{Predictive Accuracy of the Learned Operator}
\label{sec:exp_prediction}

For RQ1a, we ask whether using multi-turn history improves prediction over using only the current reading. For RQ1b, we compare the learned lifting and LSTM with KOMPAS, our linear case. The setup, tasks, and baselines are described in \S\ref{sec:exp_setup}; Table~\ref{tab:prediction} reports the skill score defined there.

KOMPAS ranks first on four of the seven task columns and second on the remaining three. Its point estimate is close to the top method in each of those three cases, trailing by no more than 0.034. Multi-turn history improves on the current reading in four columns, with paired bootstrap intervals that exclude zero. In the other three, the difference is not distinguishable from zero. The history window captures the attribute’s current level and, to a lesser extent, its trend—information unavailable from a single reading.

The learned nonlinear lifting does not outperform the identity lifting with an interval excluding zero on any of the seven columns. Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}(c) shows the per-column contrasts; \S\ref{sec:exp_mechanism} discusses a possible explanation. Compared with the LSTM, the results are mixed: the linear case performs better on Joint-3 and CEFR, while the LSTM performs better on Constraint. We do not establish a mechanism for the LSTM’s advantage on Constraint.

Overall, history improves prediction over the current reading on four of seven task columns, supporting RQ1a for those tasks. Neither the learned lifting nor the LSTM outperforms KOMPAS consistently, so the results do not support RQ1b.
