\section{Control-Line Provenance and the Equal-Budget Closed-Loop Comparison}
\label{app:A4_equal_cost}

\textbf{Control-line provenance.} The benchmark numbers of Table~\ref{tab:control} come from a pipeline separate from the modeling line.  % 04a; numbers_benchmark.yaml header
It runs two frozen target models, Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B, on the eleven subtasks of IFBench, IFEval and COLLIE, and reports each benchmark's constraint-satisfaction score as a per-subtask percentage.  % contract.yaml meta.benchmarks; numbers_benchmark.yaml unit
Base applies no control, and KOMPAS reads only the text it sends.
TMPC and RE-Control are reproductions run under the same protocol, not the original authors' releases.  % c6 notes; tab_control.tex Access column
Every cell is a single point estimate: no seeds, intervals or raw logs accompany the table, and the paper treats the numbers as reported.

\textbf{Equal-budget comparison, protocol.} On three self-built tasks the closed loop also runs against open-loop fixed schedules under the same instruction budget cap.
The comparison arm is the best of those schedules, chosen after the fact on the same data.  % CLOSED_LOOP_SYNTHESIS.md sec. 1-2; adaptive_vs_fixed_claim_plan.md
Defense sends one safety reminder per trajectory and reads the judge's safety level on turns 3 to 5; its best schedule places the reminder at turn 5.  % n_loop_015 (fixed_t5), n_loop_019; defense_table2_results.md
Constraint sends whole-paragraph or targeted reminders over twenty turns and reads constraint retention on turns 15 to 20; its best schedule sends whole-paragraph reminders on fixed turns.  % constraint_results.md S3
CEFR sends step-down instructions toward a target readability level and reads the terminal level error.
Its fixed ladder measures the level once and steps down the rounded distance to the target.  % tsar_cefr_results.md; n_loop_054, n_loop_057
Defense uses 5 seeds and 8 attack families, constraint 3 seeds and 48 items, CEFR 3 seeds and 100 items; intervals are paired bootstraps over attacks, items and items respectively.  % n_loop_019 (n=5 seeds), n_loop_040 (n=8), n_loop_048 (n=3), n_loop_058 (n=100)

\textbf{Findings.} Table~\ref{tab:equal_budget} gives the per-task numbers behind the cross-check of \S\ref{sec:exp_analysis}.
The loop ties the best schedule on constraint and CEFR, with intervals containing zero.
On defense it falls below by 0.025 under the per-attack resampling unit; under the per-trajectory unit that interval also contains zero.  % n_loop_048, n_loop_060, n_loop_040; per-trajectory CI [-0.0688, +0.0251] from defense_table2_results.md row 80
The tie is not bought with a smaller budget: KOMPAS spends less only on defense and spends more on the other two tasks.  % CLOSED_LOOP_SYNTHESIS.md sec. 1
The last column sets the scale: the instruction itself moves the reading by 0.12 to 0.14, while rescheduling the same budget moves it by at most 0.03.  % nc_2; CLOSED_LOOP_SYNTHESIS.md sec. 4
Two further checks say the gap is a property of the tasks rather than of the controller.
On defense a causal oracle fitted on the logged trajectories, with the same observables as KOMPAS, gains $+0.006$ $[-0.017, +0.029]$ over the best schedule.
A perfect controller on those readings would also tie.  % n_loop_046
On CEFR the three feedback policies within one instruction family span 0.013 in terminal error, while switching family moves it by 0.040.
The room that feedback can buy on that task is smaller than a wording choice.  % tsar_cefr_results.md D-2.5; CLOSED_LOOP_SYNTHESIS.md sec. 4 (range 0.0129, family gap 0.0395)
The defense column carries the self-judge caveat of Appendix~\ref{app:A2_small_columns}; the other two tasks use an independent judge.  % judge_kind=self on defense only (n_loop_* supersede_reason)
